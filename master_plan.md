# Summary:
Cue is intended to be a Jira replacement for organizations that have a small amount of assignees to tickets, but a large amount of requesters. Cue specifically is intended for designers or marketers (potentially IT to replace Zammad), typically for someone working in a real-estate office that will be providing small designs (files like pictures for instagram, digital copies of flyers, or advertisements) or marketing initiatives (social media posts, advertisements on reddit and other sites, things that are actionable but not as concrete). Cue will almost certainly be used to help people with managing IT problems - a requester could ask an IT person to help reset their password.

# Architecture, stack, and engineering philosophy:
Cue should be built with Ruby on Rails and React with tailwind CSS. If monorepo makes sense, then we should aim for monorepo. The platform code and application should be dead simple on its own. Our largest technical priorities will be keeping costs low with simple technical design decisions. We will focus on building for today, and leave scaling problems for tomorrow. However, our solution must be complete and thorough, easily scaling to daily active 5,000 users a month. For example, we should be able to containerize a local database, images could probably be cached in our build steps, etc. This app will be developed primarily with AI. Because of that, we need a self-improvement loop and in-depth documentation that is human readable, concise, and legitimately helpful. From the beginning, we will prioritize tests. Each line of code written must have a test and our test coverage should be perfect. We should write negative tests to enforce our designs. From the beginning, we will also use react-doctor and enforce every rule to ensure we have a score of 100. From the beginning, we need to have two themes, and allow for organizations to customize their own theme with adding a logo or defining their own colors for a theme (really just selecting a preset of colors.) Things like DB caching or avoiding N+1 queries should be built in from the start. We cannot afford poor design choices like the use of "any" types. We could find popular rails or react skills or packages and add them to our project to improve our code quality from the start.
DB architecture is debatable. We may prefer noSQL, MySQL, Postgresql, or something else. Do not make architecture decisions like this until you understand the remainder of the scope of work.

# Billing:
An organization pays for an annual or monthly license, that has multiple tiers. The "basic" tier allows for up to 15 users, 2 are board owners. The "premium" tier will allow for up to 200 users, with 10 board owners. Then there is an "enterprise" tier that is a monthly cost per user in an organization, with unlimited board owners, but a minimum of 100 users is required. Basic will start at $3.99/mo and premium will be $13.99/mo. Enterprise will be $0.15/mo per user, with a minimum of $19.99 a month. Premium and enterprise will all have the same features, but basic will not include the option to customize ticket fields for board owners. It will also have a file upload limit of 2MB per ticket, and a maximum of 50 tickets in the backlog + on all boards (not counting completed) at a time. Basic will also not be allowed to link cues to other cues, create initiatives, or have custom themes. Basic and premium have two month trials available, while enterprise has no trial available.

# Design standards:
Each piece of React code should be reusable. Our designs need to be coherent and we need to avoid one-off components unless it absolutely makes sense. As we work on the design for the frontend, we need to have a way to enforce standards. Whether that's through tests or a simple document, it's required that everything in our site feels cohesive. We can't have large variants in padding or font sizes without it making sense (headers and subtext make sense). As part of our design standards, we need guided tutorials and we need to optimize workflows. It should take very few clicks to log in and see an item/cue/ticket and manage it. In our language, it needs to be a little snarky. For example, when we show them what a "cue" is and explain it, we should acknowledge "normal people just call this a ticket; you can do that if you really want." Our designs should feature powerful tools (navigation to and from tickets when they are linked) but not so many that the UI is too cluttered like Jira. Our focus is to allow for our board organizers/owners to have complete control over their board, while remaining focused on the tasks they have been assigned. The UI must be extremely simple with minimal flair (no accented side borders) and stark colors should be part of either items that we can "color code" or buttons that are important (cancel or destructive actions are red). A hardcoded color within a component is a massive no-no, colors, styling, etc, must be cohesive and each component must match a theme. For example, we will have a light and dark mode, and then an organizational theme. This is designed specifically for designers - enforce designs and have a creative, classy design that looks like a soft, easy tool. An older style font with a cursive logo, cream papyrus colors, charcoal texturing, deep and darkened vibrant colors (like a dark scarlet), for the light theme. 

# Workflow (product) basics:
Cue is intended to be more simple version of Jira - there are no sprints or agile methodologies. There is simply a "backlog" and a "cue'd" board. An organization can have multiple "cue'd" boards but only one backlog. Each "cue'd" board is meant for an individual, or assignee. For example, Charlie has his own board. To help Charlie prioritize work, there is a maximum configurable amount of items he can have on his board, regardless of item/ticket/cue (these three all mean the same thing) status. By default, it will be 10 items. Board owners are the only ones that may move items from the backlog to their own board. We may incorporate some features on Jira that are genuinely popular, but our goal is to maintain simplicity for small design organizations to remain focused.

# Metrics and insights:
Organizations and board owners will need insights and metrics to help them refine workflows. We should point out where they are spending the most time (total time x type of cues went from in progress to done), what sort of cue types are slowest, who requests the most, etc.

# "Cues" (tickets):
Each ticket will be called a "cue" since a cue is a prompt to take an action. Each cue will have the following statuses: "cue'd" (todo), in progress, in review, resolved (with a sub status, closed, infeasible, or done), which actually moves it off to a searchable "completed" section. Board owners can control how their ticket numbers are generated for easy reference. Tickets may have comments from users (tagged users get notified via email), a stake (low, medium, high), a priority deadline, an acceptable deadline, work category, file uploads, and "billable hours". These are all optional and can be toggled by the organization or board owner, but by default, all except billable hours are included on tickets. Board owners will be allowed to set options to help them organize their work. They can manage their list of work categories, they can set how many priorities they have to choose from (for example, just low and high). Tickets can have hooks and audit logs that board owners can configure. For example, when a ticket is moved to done, a comment is required. Each board owner will have a separate board that allows them to search. Board owners may link cues to other cues, which allows easy navigation back and forth. A cue/ticket may only be modified by its board owner or original requester.

# Initiatives (sort of like Jira epics)
Initiatives will be "parents" of tickets, but an initiative functionally acts as a workspace that allows users to create files, similar to confluence. However, they may also upload and organize marketing materials. Text files will be made simplistic, with markdown support only, but images and links may be integrated. When a cue (ticket) is linked to an initiative, it will show as a badge. Clicking it brings you to the initiative, where on the left is a button that says "cues" that shows all cues linked to that initiative. Initiatives have a file structure similar to confluence. Each initiative must have a summary. An initiative is organization-wide, multiple board owners can contribute to an initiative.
